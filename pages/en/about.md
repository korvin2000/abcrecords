::: lead
*Guitarists and Composers* is an encyclopaedia of the people behind the
guitar's history: performers, composers, conductors, teachers and luthiers.
Every entry is laid out as a page of an illuminated codex — a life, a dossier,
portraits, and the documents behind them.
:::

## What this is

The project gathers and preserves biographical material on guitarists and
composers — above all the kind scattered across print editions, concert
programmes and ageing websites, and therefore easily lost.

Everything is arranged the same way. Each entry has:

- a **life** — the article itself, written in BioMD Lite;
- a **dossier** — structured facts: dates, places, instruments, genres,
  teachers and pupils;
- a **gallery** — portraits and recordings;
- **scrolls** — documents, scans and links to primary sources.

Technical pages such as this one carry no dossier: they are text only.

## History

The project was founded in 2001 by [Sergey Tavrovsky](authors.bio.md). General
direction passed to Viktor Tavrovsky in 2005. The material has been hosted at
[www.abc-guitars.com](http://www.abc-guitars.com/) since March 2007, and also
at [www.abcguitars.com](http://www.abcguitars.com/) since November 2008.

One of the first printed notices of the project appeared in the almanac
*Renaissance of the Guitar 2002* (Republic of Belarus).

A large part of the material comes from the older site
[guitar-times.ru](http://www.guitar-times.ru/) and is being brought into a
single format entry by entry.

## Languages

Entries are translated gradually, and each exists in exactly the languages it
has been written in. If an entry has no edition in your language, the codex
opens it in its original tongue and says so in the list.

Searching reaches further than writing does: names and their variants are kept
apart from the articles, so an entry that exists only in Russian can still be
found by its Latin or Chinese spelling.

## Using the codex

- Type a name into the search field — a surname or a fragment will do.
- Narrow the result with the craft and country marks.
- ← and → leaf between entries without closing the codex.
- The address of an open entry can be saved or shared: it has the form
  `#/file-name`.
